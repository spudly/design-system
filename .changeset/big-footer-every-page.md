Every page can render a BigFooter: a fat footer with a big brand wordmark, multi-column link lists, app-store links, and social icon links, supplied to each page as one optional `footer` prop object ({ brand, columns, storeLinks, socialLinks }). The footer is host-supplied: a page renders its BigFooter only when its host provides the `footer` prop, and renders no footer band when the prop is omitted — hosts adopting this release must pass footer data to get a footer. PublicPageFrame is the one exception: when its `footer` prop is omitted it builds a minimal default BigFooter from its existing contract (the brand wordmark plus a Legal column carrying Privacy and Terms). When a host does supply footer data, it can test that its brand, link columns (including a Legal column with Privacy, Terms, and "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information"), store links, and social links appear on the page. The footer sticks to the bottom of the viewport on short pages (in-flow, full-viewport-height page shell) and scrolls naturally on long pages. On narrow viewports below the 640px stack breakpoint the columns collapse to a stacked single column — brand, store links, and social links included — with no horizontal scrolling, verifiable in Storybook at a 430px mobile width. PublicPageFrame's old footer is replaced by BigFooter (PublicFrameFooter is retired), and LegalDocumentPage's `footerLinks` prop is replaced by the same `footer` object.
